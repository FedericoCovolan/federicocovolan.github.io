Transition Design Methodology

The Transition Design framework was applied as the core methodology. The process involves three interconnected phases applied to the complex challenge of sustainable supply chain management.

Key phases: Re-framing present and monitoring progress → Adapting strategies → Reflecting and learning → Waiting and observing → Prototyping solutions → Stakeholder engagement → Iterative refinement.

Analysis

To analyze the data, we used the Geels Multi-Level Perspective model as a framework for understanding the transition to a sustainable supply chain.

According to Geels, sustainability transitions can be analysed through three interconnected levels: the socio-technical landscape, the sociotechnical regime and niche-innovations. We can see this as macro, meso and micro level.

Finding Causes & Challenges

Finding Causes: Complexity of decision making · Lack of true intention/commitment · No behaviour change · Lack of confidence – fear of wrong decisions/timing · Lack of fund/budget · Expectations · Lack of long-term direction

Balancing service and sustainability — Maintaining service levels while meeting sustainability objectives.

Multi-stakeholder impact — Managing diverse and conflicting impacts across various stakeholders.

Data-sharing resistance — Overcoming active resistance to sharing critical data, especially in logistics.

Value of sustainability — Determining if sustainability has sufficient value in the business model to support required changes.
